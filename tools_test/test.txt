**The Watchmaker's Apprentice**

One. The attic clock began to weep.
Not tick—a shudder, iron sigh,
as if the gears had learned to keep
a memory of when the sky
was younger, and the brass was bright,
before the workshop lost its light.

Two. The apprentice, twelve years old,
climbed stairs the dust had claimed for years.
His lantern made the shadows fold,
and in the dark he met the gears—
a heart of wheels, a pulse of springs,
a patient song of ticking things.

Three. He wound the mainspring, slow and true,
and felt the rhythm catch and climb.
The old clock sang the whole night through,
and morning came—the first in time
the workshop knew without a lie,
the great clock waking from a dream.
